# Reflection

## Decisions

One of the most important decisions I made during this project was keeping the scope realistic. Early on, I considered adding larger features such as online ordering or reservations, but I realized those ideas would take attention away from building a polished and functional MVP. Instead, I focused on creating a strong multi-page restaurant website with consistent branding, responsive layouts, and meaningful content.

Another important decision was building around the “day versus night” concept. That idea shaped nearly every design decision in the project, from the menu structure to the colors, layout, and content. It gave the site a clear identity and helped guide the overall direction of the project throughout the semester.

## What worked

The strongest part of the project is the overall concept and consistency. The website communicates the idea clearly, and each page supports the larger restaurant experience. I am especially satisfied with the homepage and the “Now Serving” feature because it feels connected to the purpose of the site instead of being a random interaction.

I also think the project improved significantly through iteration. The contact page became much more polished after redesigning it into a “Plan Your Visit” section with an embedded map and more intentional layout structure. Small improvements in spacing, navigation, responsiveness, and organization made the final site feel more complete than the original Project 01 version.

## What I would do differently

If I started over, I would spend more time planning the structure of the site before building individual pages. As the project grew, I found myself reorganizing sections and adjusting layouts multiple times. A stronger design system and page structure from the start would have made the process more efficient.

I would also test mobile layouts earlier instead of waiting until later in development. While the site became responsive by the end, some adjustments could have been avoided if I had tested smaller screen sizes more consistently throughout the project.

Another thing I would do differently is create more reusable CSS utility classes earlier. Some sections could have been cleaner and easier to maintain if I had planned shared styles more carefully from the beginning.

## What I learned

This project taught me that web development is heavily iterative. A project can technically function early on, but refinement, usability, and polish make a major difference in how professional the final result feels. I learned that improving spacing, layout hierarchy, responsiveness, and organization can sometimes have as much impact as adding completely new features.

I also became more comfortable with GitHub Pages deployment and debugging issues between local development and the live deployed version. I learned how important it is to test links, scripts, responsive layouts, and navigation directly on the deployed site instead of only locally.

On a personal level, I learned that I enjoy projects that combine design and functionality. I liked building a project that had a clear visual identity while also thinking about usability and interaction. Overall, the project helped me become more confident with structuring multi-page websites, refining layouts, and iterating based on feedback and evaluation.
